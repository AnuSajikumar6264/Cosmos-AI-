# Cross-module couplings {#sec:couplings}

What separates this system from three models in one wrapper is that the modules exchange specific variables, and that each exchange is measured. Table [-@tbl:couplings] states each coupling, what crosses the boundary, and what the measurement shows.

{{TABLE:couplings}}

: **The four couplings and their measured effect.** Confidence intervals are 2,000 cluster bootstraps over launch families (C1), clouds (C2) or launches (C3). A positive C1 effect means the features help; a negative C3 difference means the status-aware policy lowers the Brier score. {#tbl:couplings}

**C1, debris to re-entry.** Motivation: fragments of one breakup share an origin and therefore a range of area-to-mass ratios, so the fate of catalogued siblings is evidence about an object's own lifetime. Formulation: $\varphi_F(i,t_0)$ enters the feature vector $x_i$ of the learned expert. Evaluation: the feature group is removed and the expert refitted with identical settings and seeds, and the paired difference in test pinball loss is bootstrapped over launch families. Result: the features help at origin 2016, {{re.o2016.abl.no_family.diff:+.4f}} ({{re.o2016.abl.no_family.lo:+.4f}} to {{re.o2016.abl.no_family.hi:+.4f}}), and the interval includes zero at origin 2011. Limitation: siblings are only informative once some of them have decayed, which is why the earlier origin gains less.

**C2, re-entry to debris.** Motivation: the debris environment is the sum of individual survival, so a lifetime model should reproduce the observed decline of a breakup cloud. Formulation: Eq. [-@eq:cloud]. Evaluation: predicted against catalogued fragment counts for every cloud with at least 30 fragments in a test cohort, at one to five years after its first catalogued fragment, with a bootstrap resampling whole clouds. Result: the best reading reaches a mean absolute error of about a tenth of a cloud's size, but with {{c2.n_clouds}} clouds the experts cannot be separated. Limitation: the number of clouds, not the number of fragments, sets the precision of this test.

**C3, health to re-entry.** Motivation: an operator that still controls a satellite determines its orbit, so drag physics does not predict its decay. Formulation: the status in $h_i$ selects a regime $\rho_i$; in the operator-controlled regime the drag forecast is withheld and the previous-window payload base rate is used instead. Evaluation: an as-of-date test on {{c3.n:,}} payloads below 600 km with {{c3.events}} decays in the following {{c3.horizon_days:.0f}} days, comparing status-aware and status-blind policies that use only information available at the as-of date, with intervals resampling launches. Result: drag physics over-predicts for operational payloads (mean predicted {{c3.status.operational.mean_p_physics:.3f}} against an observed rate of {{c3.status.operational.event_rate:.3f}}) and under-predicts for non-operational ones ({{c3.status.non-operational.mean_p_physics:.3f}} against {{c3.status.non-operational.event_rate:.3f}}). The status-aware policy lowers the Brier score, but the interval includes zero. Limitation: with {{c3.events}} decays in one window the test shows the direction of the effect and not its size.

**C4, health and debris to response.** Motivation: a high-risk conjunction for a spacecraft that may be unable to manoeuvre warrants escalation. Formulation: an alarm state or a non-operational status raises the response level of a high-risk event. Evaluation: none is possible, because no public dataset pairs conjunction messages with telemetry or health status for the same spacecraft. This coupling is implemented and reported as a demonstration, and it supports no performance claim anywhere in this paper.
