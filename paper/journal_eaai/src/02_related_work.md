# Related work and the research gap {#sec:related}

## Physics-based lifetime and re-entry prediction

Semi-analytic decay theory [@kinghele1987satellite; @frey2019extension] and numerical propagators with empirical atmospheres underpin operational tools such as the European Space Agency's debris-risk suite [@braun2020drama]. Assessments of the inter-agency re-entry campaigns report mean errors of about 10 % of the remaining lifetime, and a ±20 % window around the predicted epoch that covers roughly 90 % of true epochs in the final days before re-entry [@pardini2018assessing]; earlier assessments of propagator accuracy reach similar conclusions [@pardini2004accuracy; @operti2017assessing]. Thermospheric density is the dominant error source, and both its empirical modelling [@emmert2021nrlmsis; @licata2022msis] and its uncertainty [@licata2022uncertainty; @pardini2012thermospheric] are active subjects. Where orbital histories are available, the ballistic coefficient is fitted from tracking data [@gondelach2017ballistic; @sang2013estimation; @pardini2008impact], which is the practice our state-estimation step approximates from two catalogue orbits.

## Learning-based lifetime and re-entry prediction

Machine-learning studies predict the re-entry epoch or the orbital lifetime from orbital histories. Table [-@tbl:gap] summarises those we could read in full. Salmaso et al. train a sequence model on 417 Space-Track bodies and test on 15 campaign objects [@salmaso2023machine]; Lotti et al. fit a physics-informed convolutional network to 1,783 payloads and report that generalisation is "not yet fully satisfactory" [@lotti2025exploring]; Jung et al. use a recurrent network on altitude histories [@jung2021recurrent], and a physics-based backtest reports calibrated lifetime distributions for 1,283 objects but calibrates on the same backtest set [@shambaugh2026validation]. Related learning work addresses density correction [@licata2022machine], ballistic-coefficient estimation [@andaristiyan2025ballistic] and lifetime regression on orbital elements [@li2023backpropagation; @peng2020machine].

Three properties are shared by every study we could read. The datasets are one to two orders of magnitude smaller than an operational catalogue; the output is a point epoch rather than a distribution; and the training population consists of objects that have already decayed, which discards the censored majority. Our Kaplan–Meier description (Section [-@sec:ablations]) shows that this majority is {{re.o2016.km.all.censored_fraction:pct}} of one test cohort and {{re.o2011.km.all.censored_fraction:pct}} of the other. None of these studies couples lifetime prediction to conjunction assessment or to spacecraft health.

## Collision risk and the debris environment

The Kelvins collision-avoidance challenge released conjunction data messages with the final risk of each event [@uriot2021cacdataset; @uriot2022spacecraft]. The latest-risk baseline scored 0.694 on the official metric and only 12 of 97 teams beat it; the winning entry scored 0.556 [@uriot2022spacecraft]. Later entries report scores on the same split [@tulczyjew2021predicting], and a pre-registered re-evaluation found that no model beats the latest-risk forecast with confidence [@vazquez2026latest]. Other learning approaches to conjunction screening and risk include surrogate models of the encounter geometry [@acciarini2021kessler; @acciarini2024improving], sequence models over message streams [@chu2024conjunction; @chu2026conjunction], and operational studies of alert handling [@stevenson2022towards; @lidtke2019optimising]. The debris environment itself is modelled from breakup statistics [@johnson2001nasa; @kessler1978collision], and breakup families have been studied for origin attribution [@wu2023investigation; @celletti2021reconnecting]; the Kelvins "space debris: the origin" data are simulated [@efthymiopoulos2021origin; @esa2021kelvinsorigin]. We found no study that uses breakup-family membership as a predictor of fragment lifetime.

## Spacecraft telemetry anomaly detection

The public spacecraft benchmarks are the Soil Moisture Active Passive and Mars Science Laboratory channels released with telemanom [@hundman2018], the European Space Agency anomaly-detection benchmark [@kotowski2026esaadb; @esaadb_dataset] and the OPS-SAT set [@ruszczak2025opssat]. Metric design is a known hazard in this literature: point adjustment can make random detectors appear strong [@kim2022rigorous; @wu2021flawed], and range-based metrics were introduced to address it [@tatbul2018precision]. Broad surveys of time-series anomaly detection make the same point about benchmark artefacts [@schmidl2022anomaly; @paparrizos2022tsbuad; @sehili2023flawed]. We therefore report event-wise and point-wise metrics without adjustment, alongside label-free references.

## Methods from artificial intelligence

Tabular prediction has moved from gradient-boosted trees [@chen2016xgboost; @ke2017lightgbm; @prokhorenkova2018catboost], which remained competitive on tabular benchmarks [@grinsztajn2022tree], to parameter-efficient deep ensembles [@gorishniy2025tabm] and to in-context foundation models that condition on the training set at inference time rather than fitting weights to it [@hollmann2025accurate; @qu2025tabicl; @qu2026tabiclv2; @nvidia2026kumo]. For time series, pretrained forecasters are used zero-shot [@ansari2024chronos; @ansari2025chronos2]. These models are normally assessed on curated benchmark suites; we are not aware of an evaluation on an operational scientific catalogue with administrative censoring and forward-in-time drift.

Uncertainty is handled here by conformal prediction, which gives finite-sample, distribution-free coverage under exchangeability [@vovk2005algorithmic; @angelopoulos2023gentle]. Conformalised quantile regression adapts interval width to the input [@romano2019cqr], Mondrian variants condition coverage on groups [@vovk2005algorithmic], and the behaviour of the guarantee under distribution shift has been studied directly [@barber2023; @candes2023conformalized; @gibbs2021aci]. Conformal methods have been applied to space-surveillance tracking [@laxhammar2010conformal; @laxhammar2015inductive]. Averaging quantile functions is a well-studied combination rule [@lichtendahl2013average], and deep ensembles are a standard uncertainty baseline [@lakshminarayanan2017ensembles]. Censored targets connect the problem to survival analysis [@harrell1982evaluating; @barnwal2022survival]. Digital twins for space systems have been proposed mostly as architectures and reviews [@liu2024digital; @guimaraes2023taxonomy], or built for single functions such as orbit prediction [@xu2025digital] and physics-informed monitoring [@em2026physics].

## The gap this paper addresses

Three gaps follow. First, no catalogue-scale, censoring-aware, forward-in-time evaluation of learned lifetime prediction with calibrated intervals exists. Second, recent tabular and time-series foundation models have not been tested on operational space-surveillance data, where the covariate distribution drifts with the solar cycle. Third, and most relevant to an integrated system, the couplings between health, debris and re-entry are asserted in digital-twin architectures but, as far as we can establish, never measured. This paper addresses all three, and reports the couplings that do not pay off as well as those that do.
