# Datasets and preprocessing {#sec:data}

All data are public and real; provenance, checksums and licences are recorded in a manifest released with the code. Table [-@tbl:datasets] lists what is used.

{{TABLE:datasets}}

: **Datasets.** The class column follows the project's own data audit, which separates real sources from derived and simulated ones. Simulated sets held on disk, such as the Kelvins debris-origin challenge data, are not used anywhere in this paper. {#tbl:datasets}

**Catalogue.** The General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects [@mcdowell2020gcat; @mcdowell2022elements] gives, for each object, one *canonical orbit* representative of early operations with its epoch, design mass and dimensions, the parent object for fragments, and the end of the current phase with a status code that distinguishes natural decay from controlled de-orbit, landing or docking. The CelesTrak catalogue [@celestrak2026satcat] adds operational status, and the CelesTrak space-weather file provides the solar and geomagnetic indices; only observed rows for days that ended before the prediction time are read. The cohort holds {{data.cohort.rows_out:,}} objects whose canonical orbit has perigee below 2000 km, of which {{data.cohort.events:,}} decayed naturally. Figure [-@fig:data-reentry] shows the cohort's orbits, its distribution in time with the cohorts of both forecast origins, the observed lifetimes with their censoring, and the space-weather record.

![**The catalogue dataset.** (a) Canonical orbits by object class; the classes overlap heavily at these densities, and the largest is drawn first. (b) Objects per year of canonical-orbit epoch, with the training, tuning and test cohorts of both origins. (c) Observed time to natural decay, and to censoring for objects still in orbit or whose phase ended otherwise. (d) Solar flux; its 81-day mean is the model input, and the bars mark the two test periods.](artwork/Figure_1.png){#fig:data-reentry width=100%}

**Conjunctions.** The Kelvins collision-avoidance challenge data [@uriot2021cacdataset; @esa2019kelvinscac] contain anonymised conjunction data messages received by the European Space Agency between 2015 and 2019. Following the challenge, inputs are the messages released at least two days before closest approach and the target is the risk of the final message. Training events are filtered to mirror the test inputs, leaving {{cac.data.train_events_kept:,}} training events, of which {{cac.data.train_high_risk}} are high risk, and the official {{cac.data.test_events:,}} test events, of which {{cac.data.test_high_risk}} are high risk. Figure [-@fig:data-debris] shows the breakup clouds of the catalogue and the structure of the message data.

![**Debris data.** (a) Catalogued fragments in orbit over time for the four largest breakup clouds of the cohort. (b) Gabbard diagram of the largest cloud. (c) Risk sequences of 75 training events, 60 drawn at random from the low-risk events and 15 from the high-risk events, so high-risk events are deliberately oversampled. (d) The forecast target: most final risks lie at the floor.](artwork/Figure_2.png){#fig:data-debris width=100%}

**Telemetry.** The Soil Moisture Active Passive and Mars Science Laboratory channels [@hundman2018] provide {{smap.config.channels}} channels with {{smap.SMAP.ar_ridge.events:.0f}} and {{smap.MSL.ar_ridge.events:.0f}} labelled anomaly sequences respectively; the label file lists one channel twice and the two rows are merged. Figure [-@fig:data-telemetry] shows example channels with their labels.

![**Telemetry data.** Two channels of each spacecraft drawn at random: training series in grey, test series in black, labelled anomaly sequences shaded. The lower panel shows the European Space Agency benchmark around the first labelled anomaly of its test half, for the target channels it affects.](artwork/Figure_3.png){#fig:data-telemetry width=100%}

**What is not available.** Orbital-element histories at catalogue scale require an account whose user agreement forbids redistribution [@spacetrack2019agreement], and no legitimate public archive exists; the European Space Agency's debris database and its re-entry predictions also need accounts [@klinkrad1991discos; @esa2026reentry]. The re-entry module therefore predicts from a single dated orbit per object rather than from a history, which is the main structural difference between this study and operational practice.
