abstract: |
  Orbital debris analysis must forecast how long objects stay in orbit and how likely they are to collide, yet practice rests on uncalibrated physics propagators. Prior lifetime studies used a few hundred to 1,800 objects, single-epoch predictions and only decayed objects; tabular foundation models have been assessed on curated benchmarks, not censored catalogues under drift. This study proposes an artificial-intelligence architecture for the debris environment, each component measured against the practice it replaces. COSMOS-AI is a digital twin holding one state per object. Debris forecasts collision risk from conjunction messages; re-entry predicts residual lifetime by a reliability-gated mixture of experts, a learned gate weighting a Monte-Carlo decay prior against 2025–2026 tabular models (TabM; in-context foundation models TabICLv2 and Kumo-Tabular); health scores telemetry with Chronos-2 zero-shot; quantiles receive Mondrian conformal and isotonic calibration. Four couplings make this one twin, not three independent models: breakup-family statistics feed the lifetime learner, per-object survival sums into cloud forecasts, operational status selects the decay regime, and health flags escalate high-risk conjunctions. Public data throughout: {{data.cohort.rows_out:,}} catalogued objects in two forward-in-time periods, the European Space Agency collision-avoidance benchmark and NASA SMAP/MSL telemetry. In 2016–2021 the foundation models cut re-entry pinball {{imp.re.o2016.tabicl_vs_xgboost_pct:.0f}} % below the best gradient-boosted baseline and {{imp.re.o2016.tabicl_vs_physics_pct:.0f}} % below the physics expert, though tied with it in 2011–2015. Conformal intervals covered {{re.o2016.rule.physics_only_conformal90.coverage_decayed:pct}}–{{re.o2011.rule.physics_only_conformal90.coverage_decayed:pct}} of decayed objects against {{re.o2016.rule.physics_only_pm20.coverage_decayed:pct}}–{{re.o2011.rule.physics_only_pm20.coverage_decayed:pct}} for the operational ±20 % rule, designed for the final days before re-entry, and history-conditioned state estimation cut 72-day over-prediction from {{t1.by_horizon.72d.expected_over_observed_ci95.lifetime.ratio:.1f}}-fold to {{t1.by_horizon.72d.expected_over_observed_ci95.physics_history.ratio:.1f}}-fold. Collision risk improved {{imp.cac.kumo_vs_persistence_pct:.0f}} % over persistence, yet none beat the latest-risk baseline on the official metric; zero-shot telemetry detection did not beat simple detectors. Three couplings were testable — family evidence helped in one period, cloud survival reached a tenth of a cloud, the status gain was not significant — and the fourth lacks public test data. Calibrated learned fusion advances debris forecasting where the full distribution matters, and covariate availability is a leakage channel. A dated, hashed forecast enables blind verification.
